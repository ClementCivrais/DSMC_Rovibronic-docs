B5a: reaction heats and chemistry-driven recession

Verified: every gas-surface (GS) and pure-surface (PS) event of the finite-rate engine releases its 0 K heat of reaction

\[\Delta E = \sum h_f(\text{reactants}) - \sum h_f(\text{products}),\]

computed from the formation enthalpies of the frozen species database; the coverage wall recedes from the bulk ledger of its surface state, on the moving body-fitted mesh of dsmcFoamAblation and on the conjugate solid; the ablating wall splits the recombination energy with the gas as the catalytic wall does. Against: hand values from the raw database sources, exact energy and atom ledgers, and the exact solution of the 0-D rate equations.

Reaction heats

Gas neutrals take the database formation enthalpy; an ion its neutral plus the ionisation potential; a bulk element 0 (graphite: \(h_f(\mathrm{C, gas})\) = 711.749985 kJ/mol); an adsorbate \(h_f(\text{gas}) - kE_a\) of its desorption, else its bindingEnergy. An independent oracle computes every \(h_f\) from the raw sources (ATcT rows and the ground-term offsets of the frozen database):

Conjugate run with a finite-rate wall

The B8 multi-region column with a coverage wall (same mechanism, the element temperature from the solid face) and a graphite-like slab (\(\rho\) 1800, \(c_p\) 710, \(k\) 100, \(T_0\) 2000 K), 20 windows: the solid recession equals \(\sum_w v_{chem,w}\,t_{s,w}\) to 1.3e-15 and the gas-side bulk ledger \(\times\, m_C/\rho_s\) (scaled by the solid/gas window time) to 5.6e-15; the solid wall points move by the recession to 1e-12; the region energy closure is \(\le\) 2.0e-12 and the chemical ledger equals \(\sum \Delta E\,N\) to 2.1e-13.

Body-fitted recession on curved walls (2026-10-04)

The receding carbon cylinder of B5b: surface chemistry on the immersed surface (section “Finite-rate recession of a curved body”) showed that the moving body-fitted wall was exact only on a plane. It moved its points by the cumulative depth \(m_f/(\rho_s A_{f,0})\) along the current point normals, so a receding circle swept \(d\,(R - d/2)\) per unit angle instead of \(m/\rho_s\) (swept/removed 0.887 after 3.6 cells). Its site areas stayed at the initial face areas, and the converging normals folded the faces at the stagnation point. Since 2026-10-04 dsmcFoamAblation works as follows:

  • every step, the removed volume of each face (removedSolidVolume) is divided by the current face area;

  • the wall points move along the normals of the initial wall by the least-squares offset of the face planes;

  • the displacement of the step is scaled so that the swept volume (face::sweptVol) equals the removed volume, so the closure holds to round-off;

  • after every mesh motion the coverage wall’s element areas follow the faces (meshMoved, adsorbed inventory kept).

The conjugate solid’s wall motion is closed the same way. On the melting Al cylinder of B8, the solid volume lost over the removed volume goes from 0.99885 to 1.00000000. On the plane, the results of the previous section are unchanged to the printed digits.

Run it yourself

Three tutorials in tutorials/verification/gsi/ reproduce this page on one core:

  • B5a_reactionHeats: the start-up table of the 19 reaction heats against the hand values of the database sources (0.005 kJ/mol);

  • B5a_energyClosure: the energy-ledger closure of the closed column (identities to 1e-10);

  • B5a_recession0D: the moving body-fitted wall against the exact solution, scaled to 1000 steps and nEquivalentParticles 1e7 (the quick run of the review), so that it runs in under two minutes.

The body-fitted recession on a curved wall is checked by B5b_curvedRecessionBodyFitted (see B5b: surface chemistry on the immersed surface): the receding cylinder sweeps the removed volume to 1e-10 and matches the verified removed carbon, coverage, rates and depth profile.

Copy a tutorial to a run directory and type ./Allrun; check.py prints PASS or FAIL per metric, redraws the comparison in plots/ and ends with PASS: n/n metrics pass.
