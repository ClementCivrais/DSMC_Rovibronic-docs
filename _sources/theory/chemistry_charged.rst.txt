Charged-species chemistry
=========================

.. warning::

   Every charged-species reaction class in the solver —
   electron-impact ionisation, atom-atom and molecule-atom
   ionisation channels, atom-ion / atom-electron ionisation,
   charge exchange, associative ionisation in forward and
   reverse forms, and the molecule-electron / molecule-ion
   dissociation-ionisation channels — is **work in progress**.
   It has been ported into the rovibronic framework but has not
   yet been reworked, verified, or validated end-to-end.
   Quantitative results from flows with charged species must be
   treated as preliminary.

The charged-species chemistry inherits the Larsen-Borgnakke
machinery and the post-reaction redistribution rules from
:doc:`chemistry`, but requires additional care on three fronts:
the state-dependent ionisation threshold of the reacting
species, the transport of the electrons in a flow, and the
electron collisions. They are discussed below.

State-dependent ionisation threshold
------------------------------------

Unlike the ground-state ionisation potential :math:`E_{\mathrm{IP}}`
tabulated for the bulk species, the ionisation threshold seen by
a molecule in an excited rovibronic state depends on its current
internal energy:

.. math::

   E_{\mathrm{ion}}(n, v, J)
     = E_{\mathrm{IP}} - E(n, v, J).

A molecule that already carries internal energy
:math:`E(n, v, J)` requires only the complement
:math:`E_{\mathrm{IP}} - E(n, v, J)` to reach the ionisation
continuum. The consequence is that excited states contribute
disproportionately to the ionisation rate, by the same mechanism
as in dissociation (:doc:`chemistry`). Ionisation fires whenever

.. math::

   E_{\mathrm{rel}} + E_{\mathrm{int}}(Q)
     \ge E_{\mathrm{ion}}(n_P, v_P, J_P),

where :math:`P` is the ionising parcel and :math:`Q` the collision
partner. The post-ionisation available energy

.. math::

   E_{\mathrm{avail}}^{\mathrm{ion}}
     = E_{\mathrm{rel}} + E_{\mathrm{int}}(Q)
       - E_{\mathrm{ion}}(n_P, v_P, J_P)

enters the Larsen-Borgnakke redistribution exactly as for the
neutral channels.

Charge exchange
---------------

The charge-exchange reaction
:math:`\mathrm{A}^{+} + \mathrm{B} \to \mathrm{A} + \mathrm{B}^{+}`
transfers a single electron between the two heavy partners. Its
heat of reaction is read directly from the database as

.. math::

   \Delta H_{\mathrm{cx}}
     = E_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mathrm{A})
       - E_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mathrm{B}).

The available energy is then

.. math::

   E_c = E_{\mathrm{rel}}
         + E_{\mathrm{int}}(P) + E_{\mathrm{int}}(Q)
         + \Delta H_{\mathrm{cx}},

and the two products are sampled in succession through the
two-stage Larsen-Borgnakke procedure of :doc:`chemistry`. No
user-supplied parameter is required beyond the ionisation
potentials already tabulated in the species database.

Associative ionisation
----------------------

Associative ionisation
:math:`\mathrm{A} + \mathrm{B} \to \mathrm{AB}^{\star}
\to \mathrm{AB}^{+} + e^{-}` proceeds in two steps: a
hypothetical intermediate :math:`\mathrm{AB}^{\star}` is first
sampled in a rovibronic state, and if the state energy lies
above the intermediate's ionisation threshold, the intermediate
ionises. Concretely:

#. **Intermediate formation.** Compute the pooled energy
   :math:`E_c = E_{\mathrm{rel}} + E_{\mathrm{int}}(A) +
   E_{\mathrm{int}}(B) + D_0(\mathrm{AB})` and sample an
   intermediate state :math:`(n^{\star}, v^{\star}, J^{\star})`
   of :math:`\mathrm{AB}^{\star}` from Larsen-Borgnakke against
   that pool.
#. **Ionisation test.** If :math:`E_c \ge
   E_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mathrm{AB}^{\star}) -
   E(n^{\star}, v^{\star}, J^{\star})`, the intermediate ionises
   and an electron is produced; otherwise the event reverts to
   an ordinary neutral collision.
#. **Product redistribution.** When ionisation fires, the
   available energy :math:`E_c -
   E_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mathrm{AB}^{\star}) +
   E(n^{\star}, v^{\star}, J^{\star})` is distributed between
   :math:`\mathrm{AB}^{+}` and the new electron through the same
   Larsen-Borgnakke machinery.

Associative ionisation is a minor channel in most air chemistry
calculations and currently requires user-supplied QK-type rate
parameters. The reverse channel
:math:`\mathrm{AB}^{+} + e^{-} \to \mathrm{A} + \mathrm{B}` is
implemented symmetrically.

Electron population: ion-attached electrons
-------------------------------------------

A free electron is about :math:`\sqrt{m_{\mathrm{N^+}}/m_e} \approx 160`
times faster than an :math:`\mathrm{N^+}` ion at the same temperature
(:math:`6.2\times10^{5}` against :math:`3.9\times10^{3}` m/s at
10 000 K). Moved with its own velocity it leaves its ion within a few
time steps, and the gas separates its charges over distances that the
field of a real plasma forbids: the Debye length is 22 µm at
:math:`n_e = 10^{17}` m\ :sup:`-3`. The solver keeps the gas neutral
cell by cell with the ion-attached electron model of SPARTA
(``fix ambipolar``) and PICLas (``AmbipolarDiffusion``), selected in
``constant/dsmcProperties``:

.. code-block:: none

   ambipolarElectrons      true;

Every time step then runs three stages.

#. **Attachment, before the move.** The electrons of a cell are attached
   to ions of the same cell, drawn at random among the ions still
   without an electron. The electron parcel is removed and its velocity
   is stored on the ion. Only the charged parcels are visited, sorted by
   cell; there is no loop over the cells.
#. **Move.** The ion carries its electron through processor transfers,
   cyclic, symmetry and wedge patches (the electron velocity is
   transformed with the ion's), axisymmetric and cell-weight cloning or
   deletion, and outflow boundaries.
#. **Release, after the move and the weighting.** The electron is
   re-created at the position of its ion, with its own velocity, for the
   collisions and the measurements.

The electron keeps its own velocity, so the electron velocity
distribution and the electron temperature are resolved and nothing is
resampled: mass, momentum and energy are conserved to round-off.
Ionisation and recombination create and destroy electrons in the cell
of their partners, and the next attachment pairs them again.

**Walls.** An ion brings its electron to the wall. The electron is
absorbed when the wall absorbs or neutralises the ion (e.g.
``wallNeutralisation``), so the electron and ion fluxes to the wall are
equal. Otherwise it is re-emitted with the ion: mirrored by a specular
reflection, drawn from the Maxwellian at the wall temperature by a
diffuse one (SPARTA), and drawn at the face temperature for a wall model
with its own re-emission (CLL). Its energy and momentum enter the wall
fluxes (``qBF``, ``fDBF``, number and mass fluxes) of the electron
species.

**Limitations.**

* No electric field: the pair diffuses with the binary diffusion
  coefficient :math:`D_i` of the ion, i.e. the ambipolar coefficient
  :math:`D_a = D_i (1 + T_e/T_i)` without its electron-pressure term, as
  in SPARTA, PICLas and Bird's quasi-neutral scheme. The model does not
  apply in a sheath or at a plasma/neutral-gas interface.
* An electron left without an ion in its cell (an inflow that injects
  both species, or an initial state that is not neutral cell by cell)
  moves with its own velocity until it shares a cell with an ion
  without an electron. The counts are printed at every terminal output::

      Ion-attached electrons (last move)      = 4800, without an ion = 0, ions without an electron = 0, dropped = 0 (0 J)

* Immersed surfaces (implicit or explicit) treat the charged species as
  the walls do: an ion is neutralised there and its attached electron
  absorbed with it (see *Immersed surfaces* below).

**Immersed surfaces.** An ion :math:`\mathrm{X^+}` hitting an implicit or
explicit immersed surface is neutralised,
:math:`\mathrm{X^+ + e^-(surface) \to X}`, as ``wallNeutralisation`` with
:math:`\beta = 1` and no work function: it leaves as its neutral (same
mass), re-emitted by the surface's re-emission (diffuse at the surface
temperature in probability mode, the default kernel of a mechanism, the
internal state drawn at that temperature), and the ionisation potential of
:math:`\mathrm{X}` goes to the surface heat (and ``eChem``). The neutral
must be in ``typeIdList`` (the ion's name less its ``+``). A reaction of the
probability list that applies to the ion (e.g. a ``wallNeutralisation`` with
:math:`\beta < 1`) replaces this default. The electron attached to the ion
is absorbed with a neutralised or absorbed ion, its kinetic energy and
momentum to the surface, else re-emitted with the ion from the Maxwellian
at the surface temperature. An electron parcel that reaches the surface
(``ambipolarElectrons false``, or an electron without an ion in its cell) is
absorbed. The surface state line gives the totals::

    ... ionsNeutralised: 3.5425e13 electronsAbsorbed: 3.5425e13 chargeToSurface: 0 neutralisationEnergy: ... electronEnergy: ... electronMomentum: (...)

Neutral species are unchanged (the same random draws, bit-identical
results).

**Start-up guard.** A case whose ``typeIdList`` holds the electron
species on a mesh of more than one cell stops at the first time step
unless ``ambipolarElectrons`` is set: ``true`` for the ion-attached
electrons, ``false`` for electrons moved ballistically with their own
velocity. Single-cell reactors keep the previous behaviour bit for bit
when the keyword is absent.

**Inlets, fills and weighting clones** (2026-11-01). With
``ambipolarElectrons true`` every ion placed by an initial fill
(``dsmcMeshFill``, ``dsmcMeshFieldFill``, ``dsmcZoneFill``) or injected by an
inflow boundary brings its electron: a fill places the electron at the
ion's position, an inlet attaches it to the ion, both with a velocity drawn
from the Maxwellian of the fill or stream (temperature and bulk velocity).
The electron flux of a neutral stream is the ion flux, and every cell is
neutral from the first step. ``E-`` listed in a fill or an inlet is then a
FatalError. A parcel cloned by the cell or axisymmetric weighting carries a
copy of its ion's attached electron.

Verification
~~~~~~~~~~~~

Scripts, cases and results are in ``run/1D/ambipolar_electrons``
(``make_cases.py``, ``run_ws.sh``, ``analyse.py``; summary in
``summary.txt``).

* **Single-cell reactors unchanged.** Without the keyword, six charged
  reactors (N + O associative ionisation, NO\ :sup:`+` + e\ :sup:`-`
  recombination, N\ :sub:`2` + e\ :sup:`-`, N + O\ :sup:`+` charge
  exchange, air-11 adiabatic and isothermal) give bit-identical initial
  states, final clouds and logs after 200 steps with a fixed seed. The
  seven melting tutorials are bit-identical too.
* **Electrons follow the ions.** A closed 20 cm tube of Ar at 3000 K
  holds a 4 cm slab of N\ :sup:`+` and e\ :sup:`-`
  (:math:`1.2\times10^{20}` m\ :sup:`-3`) at a uniform heavy-particle
  density of :math:`10^{21}` m\ :sup:`-3`, without chemistry. With the
  attachment, :math:`N(e^-) = N(\mathrm{N^+})` in every cell at every
  write, also on four processors and after a restart, and the total
  energy is constant to the twelve printed digits. The ions spread with
  :math:`D = 0.989\,D_{\mathrm{CE}}`, where
  :math:`D_{\mathrm{CE}} = 2.945` m\ :sup:`2`/s is the first
  Chapman-Enskog coefficient of the frozen N\ :sup:`+`-Ar VSS pair;
  the same ions without electrons give :math:`0.987\,D_{\mathrm{CE}}`.
  With ``ambipolarElectrons false`` the electrons fill the tube within
  two time steps.

.. figure:: ../_static/figures/ambipolar/A_variance_attached.png
   :width: 60%

   Variance of the ion and electron positions in the closed tube:
   analytical solution (Chapman-Enskog :math:`D`, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck
   initial layer), ions without electrons (baseline) and the present
   attached electrons (two replicas, four processors, restarted run).

.. figure:: ../_static/figures/ambipolar/A_profiles_off.png
   :width: 60%

   Ballistic electrons (``ambipolarElectrons false``): at 40, 120 and
   200 µs the electrons are spread over the whole tube while the ions
   follow the analytical solution.

* **Pairs created and destroyed.** A periodic box of N and O
  (:math:`10^{22}` m\ :sup:`-3`) with NO\ :sup:`+` and e\ :sup:`-`
  (:math:`1.2\times10^{21}` m\ :sup:`-3`) at 10 000 K runs associative
  ionisation and dissociative recombination with product creation. One
  cell without attachment, one cell with it and 20 cells with it give
  the same NO\ :sup:`+` history and rate coefficients
  (:math:`k_f = 1.30`-:math:`1.31\times10^{-16}` m\ :sup:`3`/s,
  :math:`k_r = 1.26`-:math:`1.28\times10^{-13}` m\ :sup:`3`/s, within
  the scatter of two replicas), with :math:`N(e^-)` equal to the ion
  count in every cell at every write.

.. figure:: ../_static/figures/ambipolar/B_NOp_history.png
   :width: 60%

   NO\ :sup:`+` number density in the periodic box: one cell without
   attachment (baseline), one cell and 20 cells with ion-attached
   electrons; the electron density of the 20-cell box coincides with
   the ion density.

* **Neutralising walls.** In a 5 cm tube between walls at 3000 K that
  neutralise N\ :sup:`+` (:math:`\beta = 1`) the electrons are absorbed
  with their ions: :math:`N(e^-) = N(\mathrm{N^+})` at every write,
  :math:`N(\mathrm{N^+}) + N(\mathrm{N})` is conserved, and the decay
  matches the ions-only baseline.

.. figure:: ../_static/figures/ambipolar/C_wall_decay.png
   :width: 60%

   Ion and electron counts between neutralising walls, with the
   ions-only baseline.

* **Immersed surfaces.** A periodic 1 cm box of Ar (:math:`10^{21}`
  m\ :sup:`-3`, 3000 K) with a slab of N\ :sup:`+` and e\ :sup:`-`
  (:math:`1.2\times10^{20}` m\ :sup:`-3`) and an immersed sphere
  (radius 2.5 mm, 3000 K), implicit and explicit (STL, also on two
  processors): :math:`N(e^-) = N(\mathrm{N^+})` in every cell at every
  write, every absorbed electron matched by a neutralised ion, the
  released energy equal to :math:`IP(\mathrm{N})` times the ions lost
  from the gas, and charge, mass, energy (gas, ionisation energy of the
  ions and surface heat) and momentum (with the surface loads) closed to
  round-off (relative :math:`10^{-14}`). With a ``wallNeutralisation``
  of :math:`\beta = 0.5` in the probability list, the ions that return
  as ions keep an electron. Neutral immersed cases are bit-identical.

.. figure:: ../_static/figures/immersed_charged/neutralisation_energy.png
   :width: 60%

   Energy released at the immersed sphere by the neutralisations:
   ionisation potential of N times the ions lost from the gas (lines)
   and the surface tally (present).

.. figure:: ../_static/figures/immersed_charged/charge_counts.png

Electron collisions
-------------------

* **No electron-electron collisions.** ``noTimeCounter`` and
  ``majorantCollisionFrequency`` skip the electron-electron pairs.
* **Sub-cycling.** ``noTimeCounter`` sub-cycles the collisions with an
  electron per cell (see :doc:`collision_sampling`); the former
  ``noTimeCounterSubCycled`` was removed on 2026-11-13.  The
  microscopic ion recombination runs from its hooks only: another
  collision-rate model stops at start-up.
* **Energy redistribution.** The Larsen-Borgnakke internal-energy
  redistribution of :doc:`larsen_borgnakke` acts on the heavy partner
  only; the electron enters with its own velocity.

Status
------

The entire charged-species block is flagged as work in progress
in :doc:`../classes/reactions`. The present page reflects the
intended physics and the current implementation; discrepancies
between the two, if any, will be resolved as the classes are
reworked and validated. The immediate next steps include:

* re-deriving the QK activation parameters for each charged
  channel from QCT or experimental rates;
* running the three-phase validation protocol on a
  re-entry-representative charged case;
* testing the field-free ion-attached electrons against an
  ambipolar-field (Boltzmann-electron) or fully kinetic electron model
  in a controlled setting.
