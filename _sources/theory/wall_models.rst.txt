Wall and inflow models
======================

The present section documents the physical models attached to
domain boundaries. Four families are distinguished —  specular,
diffuse, Cercignani-Lampis-Lord, and rovibronic-aware —
together with the accommodation coefficients and the treatment of
the internal degrees of freedom at reflection.

Specular reflection
-------------------

A specular wall reverses the velocity component normal to the
patch face and leaves the tangential components unchanged,

.. math::

   \mathbf{v}' = \mathbf{v} - 2\,(\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}})\,
                 \hat{\mathbf{n}},

with :math:`\hat{\mathbf{n}}` the outward face normal. The
internal state of the parcel is unaffected, and no momentum,
energy, or mass is exchanged with the wall. Specular walls are
primarily of use in symmetry planes, periodic surrogates, and
free-molecular verification.

Fully diffuse reflection
------------------------

The Maxwellian diffuse wall thermalises the reflected parcel
with the wall. After reflection, the velocity is drawn from the
half-Maxwellian at the wall temperature :math:`T_w`,

.. math::

   f_w(\mathbf{v}) \propto
     (\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}_w)\;
     \exp\!\left[-\frac{m\,|\mathbf{v} - \mathbf{v}_w|^{2}}
                      {2\,k_B\,T_w}\right],
   \qquad \mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}_w > 0,

with :math:`\mathbf{v}_w` the (usually zero) wall velocity and
:math:`\hat{\mathbf{n}}_w` the inward-pointing face normal. In
the continuous-internal-energy implementation the internal
energy is redrawn from a Boltzmann distribution at :math:`T_w`;
the rovibronic-aware variant discussed below redraws the
quantum state :math:`(n, v, J)` instead.

Mixed diffuse / specular
------------------------

The Maxwell mixed model introduces an accommodation coefficient
:math:`\alpha \in [0, 1]`: a fraction :math:`\alpha` of incoming
parcels reflect diffusely, the remainder specularly. In the
implemented variant ``dsmcMixedDiffuseSpecularWallPatch``,
:math:`\alpha` is a single scalar; the field variant
``dsmcMixedDiffuseSpecularWallFieldPatch`` allows :math:`\alpha`
and :math:`T_w` to vary patch face by patch face.

Cercignani-Lampis-Lord (CLL) scattering
---------------------------------------

The CLL model [Lord1991wa]_ offers an anisotropic alternative to
the fully-diffuse Maxwellian kernel, with two accommodation
coefficients :math:`\alpha_n` and :math:`\alpha_t` for the
normal and tangential components of the momentum. Writing
:math:`V_n, V_t` for the normal and tangential velocity
magnitudes scaled by :math:`\sqrt{2 k_B T_w / m}`, the CLL
kernel samples post-collision :math:`V_n', V_t'` from

.. math::

   V_n' = \sqrt{(1 - \alpha_n)\,V_n^{2}
                + 2\,\sqrt{(1 - \alpha_n)\,\alpha_n}\,V_n\,V_r\cos\theta
                + \alpha_n\,V_r^{2}},

.. math::

   V_t' = (1 - \alpha_t)\,V_t + \sqrt{\alpha_t\,(2 - \alpha_t)}\,V_r',

with :math:`V_r, V_r'` Maxwell-Boltzmann-distributed thermal
speeds and :math:`\theta` uniform on :math:`[0, 2\pi)`. The two
limits :math:`\alpha_n = \alpha_t = 0` (specular) and
:math:`\alpha_n = \alpha_t = 1` (fully diffuse) are recovered
from the same kernel.

Rovibronic-aware wall: ``dsmcMixedDiffuseSpecularWallRotPatch``
----------------------------------------------------------------

The rovibronic-aware wall is the only boundary class that samples
on the quantum-state space at reflection. It behaves like the
Maxwell mixed model for the velocity, but replaces the internal-
energy redistribution by a Boltzmann draw over the species'
rovibronic levels:

.. math::

   P(n, v, J)
     = \frac{g(n, v, J)\,\exp\!\bigl[-E(n, v, J)/k_B\,T_w\bigr]}
            {\displaystyle\sum_{n',v',J'}
              g(n', v', J')\,\exp\!\bigl[-E(n', v', J')/k_B\,T_w\bigr]},

with :math:`T_w` the wall temperature. The partition function in
the denominator is the full internal partition function of
:doc:`rovibronic_levels`. This is the right choice for flows in
which the internal populations impinging on the wall carry
information (radiation, catalysis, non-equilibrium boundary
layers) and must be equilibrated with the wall on reflection.

Free-stream inflow
------------------

The free-stream inflow patch injects parcels drawn from a
Maxwellian distribution at prescribed density :math:`n_\infty`,
temperature :math:`T_\infty`, and drift velocity
:math:`\mathbf{v}_\infty`. The number of particles entering the
domain over a patch face of area :math:`A_f` during
:math:`\Delta t` follows from the half-range flux integral:

.. math::

   \dot{N}_{\mathrm{in}}
     = \frac{n_\infty\,A_f\,\Delta t}{\sqrt{2\pi\,m/k_B T_\infty}}\,
       \bigl[\exp(-s^{2}) + \sqrt{\pi}\,s\,(1 + \mathrm{erf}\,s)\bigr],

with :math:`s = (\mathbf{v}_\infty\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}_w) /
\sqrt{2 k_B T_\infty/m}` the speed ratio. Sampled velocities
follow the acceptance-rejection construction of Bird.

Chapman-Enskog inflow
---------------------

The ``dsmcChapmanEnskogFreeStreamInflowPatch`` extends the
Maxwellian inflow with a first-order Chapman-Enskog correction
accounting for a non-zero stress and heat flux at the patch
face,

.. math::

   f(\mathbf{v}) = f_{\mathrm{MB}}(\mathbf{v})\,
                   \Bigl[1 + \tfrac{2}{5}\,\tilde{\mathbf{q}}\cdot\mathbf{c}\,
                              (\mathbf{c}^{2} - 5/2)
                       + \tilde{\boldsymbol{\tau}} : \mathbf{c}\,\mathbf{c}
                   \Bigr],

with :math:`\mathbf{c}` the peculiar velocity in units of the
most-probable speed, and :math:`\tilde{\mathbf{q}},
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\tau}}` the scaled heat-flux and stress
tensors. This is the natural inflow when coupling DSMC to a
continuum upstream, where non-zero Navier-Stokes-like
first-order moments characterise the state of the gas.

Pressure and mass-flow inlets
-----------------------------

Pressure- and mass-flow-driven inlets
(``dsmcWangPressureInlet``, ``dsmcLiouFangPressureInlet``,
``dsmcMassFlowRateInlet``) apply Maxwellian sampling at an
inferred density chosen so that the desired pressure or the
desired mass flow rate is respected in the long-time average.
They are the natural choice when the upstream reservoir is
better characterised through :math:`p_0` or :math:`\dot{m}`
than through :math:`n_\infty`.

Particle deletion
-----------------

Parcels that reach a ``dsmcDeletionPatch`` are removed from the
simulation. The patch supports either the ``allSpecies`` switch
or a ``typeIds`` list, so that outflow patches may be made
species-selective when required.

Cyclic boundaries
-----------------

The ``dsmcCyclicBoundaries`` block in ``boundariesDict`` supports
periodic pairing. The
``dsmcReflectiveParticleMembranePatch`` variant reflects a
user-specified fraction of incoming parcels back into the domain
while letting the rest pass through; this is the mechanism used,
for instance, to model permeable membranes in micro-nano-flow
applications.

Gas-surface interaction (catalysis and ablation)
================================================

Beyond the passive scattering kernels above, the solver provides a
family of **reactive walls** for thermal-protection-system (TPS)
modelling: finite-rate catalysis, finite-rate carbon ablation,
adsorption-site coverage chemistry, and vapour-pressure
sublimation. The reactive walls are run-time-selected patch classes
—  ``dsmcCatalyticWallPatch``, ``dsmcAblatingWallPatch`` and
``dsmcCoverageWallPatch`` —  that share a common surface-reaction
infrastructure (``surfaceReaction`` and its derivatives
``srRecombination`` and ``srFiniteRate``); their dictionary
interface is documented in :doc:`../classes/boundaries` and
:doc:`../guide/input_reference`.

Finite-rate surface reactions
-----------------------------

Every per-impact surface reaction is characterised by a
dimensionless reaction probability of generalised Arrhenius form

.. math::

   \beta_i(T_w) = \min\!\bigl(1,\; A\,T_w^{\,n}\,
                              e^{-T_a/T_w}\bigr),

with :math:`A` dimensionless, :math:`n` a temperature exponent, and
:math:`T_a = E_a/R` the activation temperature. On each wall impact
the probabilities of the competing reactions are summed; with
probability :math:`\min(\sum_i \beta_i, 1)` one reaction is selected
by cumulative branching, the reactant is consumed, the gas-phase
product is re-emitted from the wall as a half-Maxwellian at
:math:`T_w`, and (for ablation) one or more solid carbon atoms are
debited from a per-face ledger.

Carbon ablation
---------------

The canonical carbon-ablation set, used for the Mars and Earth
heat-shield cases, comprises the oxidation, Boudouard and
nitridation channels:

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   \mathrm{O}  + \mathrm{C(s)} &\to \mathrm{CO},
     &\beta &= 0.63\,e^{-1160/T_w} && \text{[Park 1976]}, \\
   \mathrm{O}_2 + \mathrm{C(s)} &\to \mathrm{CO} + \mathrm{O},
     &\beta &= 0.50, \\
   \mathrm{CO}_2 + \mathrm{C(s)} &\to 2\,\mathrm{CO},
     &\beta &= 0.9 && \text{[Zhluktov-Abe 1999]}, \\
   \mathrm{N}  + \mathrm{C(s)} &\to \mathrm{CN},
     &\beta &= 0.30 && \text{[Park-Bogdanoff]}.
   \end{aligned}

The oxidation probability is the classic Park line-of-centres
form [Park1976gsi]_; the CO\ :sub:`2` Boudouard channel uses the
Zhluktov-Abe [ZhluktovAbe1999]_ efficiency. **Titan nitridation**
(N + C(s) → CN) reuses the same machinery, with the temperature-
independent Park rate :math:`\beta = 0.30` as the default and an
opt-in low-rate Zhang-type correlation
:math:`\beta = 0.0791\,e^{-5663/T_w}` reflecting the order-of-
magnitude scatter in the measured nitridation efficiency.

Finite-rate catalysis with :math:`\gamma(T)`
--------------------------------------------

Catalytic recombination (e.g. O + O → O\ :sub:`2`,
N + N → N\ :sub:`2`) is modelled through a recombination
coefficient :math:`\gamma`, the probability that an impinging atom
leaves as half of a recombined molecule. Three forms are supported
through ``srRecombination``: a constant ``gamma``; an Arrhenius
:math:`\gamma(T_w) = \gamma_A\,T_w^{\,n}\,e^{-T_a/T_w}`; and a
tabulated piecewise-linear ``gammaTable``. Material correlations
are provided for reinforced carbon-carbon (RCC), reaction-cured
glass / SiO\ :sub:`2`, and silicon carbide (SiC), after Stewart
[Stewart1997]_ and Scott [Scott1983]_.

The **Kisliuk precursor-mediated sticking** model is available on
the coverage wall,

.. math::

   S(\theta) = \frac{S_0}{1 + K\,\theta/(1 - \theta)},

which reduces to the Langmuir form :math:`S = S_0(1 - \theta)` for
:math:`K = 1`. Each recombination event releases an energy
:math:`E_{\mathrm{rec}}`, of which a fraction :math:`\beta_{\mathrm{acc}}`
is accommodated to the wall and :math:`(1 - \beta_{\mathrm{acc}})`
is returned to the nascent molecule as extra kinetic energy; this
**recombination-energy accommodation** lets the catalytic
heat load be partitioned between the surface and the gas.

Coverage chemistry
------------------

The ``dsmcCoverageWallPatch`` carries a per-species surface
coverage :math:`\theta` (occupied fraction of a finite site
inventory) and resolves four elementary surface processes:

* **Langmuir adsorption** —  :math:`\mathrm{A(g)} + * \to
  \mathrm{A(s)}` with sticking :math:`S = S_0(1 - \theta)`
  (dissociative adsorption carries an extra :math:`(1 - \theta)`);
* **Polanyi-Wigner desorption** with lateral interactions —
  :math:`k_{\mathrm{des}} = \nu_0\,10^{C_b \theta}\,
  \exp\!\bigl[-(E_0 + W\theta)/T_w\bigr]`, so that both the
  pre-exponential and the desorption energy depend on coverage;
* **Eley-Rideal** —  :math:`\mathrm{A(g)} + \mathrm{B(s)} \to
  \mathrm{AB(g)} + *`, per impact with probability
  :math:`\gamma_{\mathrm{ER}}\,\theta_B`;
* **Langmuir-Hinshelwood** —  :math:`\mathrm{A(s)} + \mathrm{B(s)}
  \to \mathrm{AB(g)} + 2*`, with rate
  :math:`k_{\mathrm{LH}}\theta_A\theta_B` and
  :math:`k_{\mathrm{LH}} = A_{\mathrm{LH}}\exp(-E_{\mathrm{LH}}/T_w)`.

Vapour-pressure sublimation and the surface energy balance
----------------------------------------------------------

At high wall temperature the solid sublimes into the carbon-vapour
species C, C\ :sub:`2` and C\ :sub:`3`. The Langmuir-Knudsen
evaporation flux of species :math:`k` is

.. math::

   \Phi_k = \frac{\alpha\,p_{\mathrm{vap},k}(T_w)}
                 {\sqrt{2\pi\,m_k\,k_B\,T_w}},
   \qquad
   p_{\mathrm{vap},k}(T_w) = a_k\,e^{-b_k/T_w},

with :math:`\alpha` an evaporation coefficient and
:math:`(a_k, b_k)` the Clausius-Clapeyron constants of the
three-species carbon-vapour fit. The corresponding number of real
molecules is injected from the surface each step, debiting the
solid carbon inventory. When radiative equilibrium is requested,
the wall temperature is solved from the running-mean convective
load against the grey-body re-radiation,
:math:`q_{\mathrm{conv}} = \varepsilon\,\sigma\,T_w^{4}`, and
relaxed toward
:math:`T_w^{\mathrm{eq}} = (Q_{\mathrm{conv}}/\varepsilon\sigma)^{1/4}`.

Surface recession
=================

The reactive walls above report a recession rate as a scalar but
do not move the mesh. Geometric surface recession is instead
handled by a **per-cell immersed level-set** method, in which a
signed-distance field :math:`\phi` (positive in the gas, negative
in the solid) is carried on the *fixed* mesh, and the
:math:`\phi = 0` isosurface recedes through the cells as carbon is
removed. This decouples the recession from the mesh topology and
works uniformly in 1D, 2D and 3D.

* **Initialisation.** For analytic shapes (plane, sphere,
  cylinder) :math:`\phi` is the signed distance to the surface;
  for arbitrary geometry it is initialised from an **STL** triangulated
  surface (a re-entry capsule or a cone at angle of attack), using
  inside/outside tests against the triangulation. Empty (1D/2D)
  patches must carry the ``empty`` boundary condition so that the
  level-set gradient is well defined.

* **Reflection and reaction.** A parcel that crosses
  :math:`\phi = 0` onto the solid side is diffuse-reflected at the
  wall temperature, optionally reacts with surface probability,
  and is mirrored back into the gas. The per-cell reaction tally
  drives the recession.

* **Level-set advance.** The receded depth per cell,
  :math:`\Delta = N_{\mathrm{abl}}\,m_{\mathrm{C}}/
  (\rho_{\mathrm{solid}}\,A_c)`, is added to :math:`\phi`, and the
  gradient is recomputed; an optional reinitialisation step
  restores :math:`|\nabla\phi| = 1`. On an STL-initialised capsule
  at angle of attack the windward face recedes more than an order
  of magnitude faster than the leeward face, as expected.

PATO loose coupling
-------------------

For coupling to a material-response solver, the immersed surface
exposes a loose, file-based interface to **PATO** (the
Porous-material Analysis Toolbox). The DSMC side exports the
boundary-layer edge state in the tabulated :math:`B'` boundary-
condition form that PATO consumes, and imports the wall
temperature, recession and species blowing returned by PATO. The
exchange is conformant in the per-cell level-set sense —  PATO
returns the surface state, while the level set carries the geometry
—  and is the intended route to a fully coupled material-response
simulation.

.. note::

   The PATO coupling is implemented as a file-exchange interface
   and has been exercised in a mock harness only; a production
   coupled run requires the :math:`B'` edge-state inversion and the
   per-species blowing map to be finalised. It should be regarded
   as a preview capability.
