Neutral chemistry
=================

The present section derives the reactive channels of
**neutral-species chemistry** as implemented in the solver:
the Quantum-Kinetic (QK) model, its Total-Collision-Energy (TCE)
ancestor, and the post-reaction redistribution of internal
energy. Charged-species reactions, which inherit the same
statistical machinery but require additional care around the
electron population, are treated separately in
:doc:`chemistry_charged`.

Total-Collision-Energy (TCE) model
-----------------------------------

The TCE model is implemented as the ``dissociationTCE`` reaction
class (dissociation channel :math:`AB + M \rightarrow A + B + M`),
selected with ``reactionModel dissociationTCE;``. It is Bird's
classic total-collision-energy chemistry model and serves both as
a production dissociation model and as the historical baseline
against which the rovibronic QK model of the next section is
compared.

Bird's TCE model [Bird1994ch]_ computes the reaction probability
from the collision energy :math:`E_c`, the activation energy
:math:`E_a`, and an experimental Arrhenius rate
:math:`k(T) = \Lambda\, T^{\eta}\,\exp(-E_a/k_B T)`. Starting from
the equilibrium rate constant

.. math::

   k^{\mathrm{eq}}(T)
     = \int_{E_a}^{\infty}
       P_{\mathrm{reac}}^{\mathrm{TCE}}(E_c)\,
       \sigma_T(E_c)\, g(E_c)\,
       f^{\mathrm{eq}}(E_c; T)\,
       \mathrm{d}E_c,

and matching to the Arrhenius form for a VHS gas (Bird eq. 6.8)
yields the closed-form per-collision reaction probability
:math:`P = \sigma_R/\sigma_T`, evaluated for every pair already
accepted by the VHS acceptance-rejection test:

.. math::

   P_{\mathrm{reac}}^{\mathrm{TCE}}(E_c)
     = C_1\,(E_c - E_a)^{C_2}
       \left(1 - \frac{E_a}{E_c}\right)^{\bar\zeta + 3/2 - \omega}
       \,\Theta(E_c - E_a),

where :math:`\Theta` is the Heaviside step, :math:`\bar\zeta` is
the average number of internal degrees of freedom of the colliding
pair (here the internal shape-parameter sum,
:math:`\bar\zeta = \tfrac{1}{2}(\zeta_{AB} + \zeta_M)`),
:math:`\omega` is the pair-averaged VHS viscosity exponent, and the
two coefficients are derived analytically:

.. math::

   C_2 = \eta - 1 + \omega,

.. math::

   C_1 = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\epsilon\,\Lambda}{2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}}
         \,\frac{\Gamma(\bar\zeta + 5/2 - \omega)}
                {\Gamma(\bar\zeta + \eta + 3/2)}
         \,\sqrt{\frac{m_r}{2 k_B T_{\mathrm{ref}}}}
         \,\frac{T_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\,1 - \omega}}
                {k_B^{\,\eta - 1 + \omega}} .

Here :math:`\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}} = \pi d_{PQ}^2` is the pair VHS
reference cross-section, :math:`T_{\mathrm{ref}}` the collision
reference temperature, :math:`m_r` the reduced mass, and
:math:`\epsilon = 2` for like reactants, :math:`1` otherwise. The
collision energy :math:`E_c` pools the relative translational
energy and the internal energy of *both* collision partners.
:math:`C_1`, :math:`C_2`, :math:`E_a` and :math:`\bar\zeta` are
constants for a given reaction and are pre-computed once.

The TCE model is a **global** reaction model: every collision
above :math:`E_a` reacts with the same probability regardless of
the partition of :math:`E_c` between the modes. This is its virtue
(only the Arrhenius triple is required) and its limitation (it
cannot, on its own, describe the influence of excited-state
populations on the reaction rate — the deficiency addressed by the
Bias model below).

The dictionary interface reads the Arrhenius coefficients directly
(:math:`\Lambda` as ``preExponentialFactor`` in m\ :sup:`3`/s,
:math:`\eta` as ``temperatureExponent``, and the activation
*temperature* :math:`E_a/k_B` in Kelvin as
``activationTemperature``); ``zetaBar`` may optionally override the
computed :math:`\bar\zeta`. The implementation has been verified to
reproduce the input :math:`k(T)` to better than 0.1 % over
5000–20000 K by numerical integration of the equation above.

Quantum-Kinetic (QK) model — full derivation
--------------------------------------------

The QK model of Bird [Bird2011qk]_, in its rovibronic extension
adopted here [Civrais2024ChemI_ch]_, replaces the TCE activation
energy by a **state-dependent threshold** for each reactant
:math:`(n, v, J)` triple. The derivation proceeds in four steps.

**Step 1 — microscopic reaction probability.** We start from the
principle that the reaction can only proceed when the collision
energy exceeds the state-specific dissociation energy
:math:`E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(n,v,J)}`. For a VHS cross section the
post-threshold reaction probability derives from the classical
line-of-centres model:

.. math::

   P_{\mathrm{reac}}^{\mathrm{QK}}(E_c; n, v, J)
     = \left(1 - \frac{E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(n,v,J)}}{E_c}\right)^{3/2 - \omega}
       \Theta\!\bigl(E_c - E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(n,v,J)}\bigr),

where :math:`E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(n, v, J)} = D_0 - E(n, v, J)`
is the energy needed to break the bond *given* that the molecule
already carries internal energy :math:`E(n, v, J)`.

**Step 2 — state-resolved reaction rate.** Integrating the above
against the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution of collision energies
for a VHS gas,

.. math::

   f^{\mathrm{eq}}(E_c; T)
     = \frac{E_c^{3/2 - \omega}}
            {(k_B T)^{5/2 - \omega}\,\Gamma(5/2 - \omega)}\,
       \exp\!\left(-\frac{E_c}{k_B T}\right),

yields the state-resolved rate

.. math::

   k^{\mathrm{QK}}_{nvJ}(T)
     = H(T)\,
       \Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{5}{2} - \omega,\;
         \frac{E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(n, v, J)}}{k_B T}\right),

where :math:`H(T)` is the VHS binary-rate kernel

.. math::

   H(T) = \sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{VHS}}\,
          \sqrt{\frac{8\,k_B T}{\pi\,\mu_{12}}}\,
          \left(\frac{T}{T_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)^{1/2 - \omega}
          \frac{1}{\Gamma(5/2 - \omega)},

and :math:`\Gamma(s, x)` is the upper incomplete gamma function.

**Step 3 — Boltzmann-weighted total rate.** Averaging over the
reactant's internal-state population yields the macroscopic rate

.. math::

   k^{\mathrm{QK}}(T)
     = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{n, v, J} g(n, v, J)\,
             e^{-E(n,v,J)/k_B T}\,
             H(T)\,\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{5}{2} - \omega,\;
               \tfrac{E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(n,v,J)}}{k_B T}\right)}
            {Q_{\mathrm{int}}(T)},

with :math:`Q_{\mathrm{int}}(T)` the internal partition function
(see :doc:`rovibronic_levels`). The summand makes manifest that
excited states contribute disproportionately to the rate, because
their dissociation threshold :math:`D_0 - E(n, v, J)` is smaller
than the ground-state value :math:`D_0`.

**Step 4 — acceptance-rejection at the collision.** At run-time,
each collision is tested against the QK probability

.. math::

   P_{\mathrm{reac}}^{\mathrm{QK}}(E_c; n_P, v_P, J_P)
     = \left(1 - \frac{E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(n_P, v_P, J_P)}}{E_c}\right)^{3/2 - \omega}
       \Theta\!\bigl(E_c - E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(n_P, v_P, J_P)}\bigr),

drawn for the dissociating parcel :math:`P` only; the partner
parcel :math:`Q` merely supplies its share of the collision
energy. If the probability exceeds the uniform random draw, the
reaction fires and the post-reaction energy redistribution begins.

Vibrationally-favored Bias model
--------------------------------

The plain TCE model weights all internal energy equally and so
underestimates the role of vibrational excitation in dissociation.
The **Bias model** of Gimelshein and Wysong [GimelsheinBias2017]_,
which descends from the vibrational-favoring cross-sections of
Wadsworth and Wysong [WadsworthWysong1997]_, restores the
state-specific behaviour by an explicit favoring factor. It is
implemented as the ``dissociationBias`` reaction class, selected
with ``reactionModel dissociationBias;`` for the channel
:math:`AB + M \rightarrow A + B + M`.

For a collision pair already accepted by the VHS test, the
dissociation probability of the molecule :math:`AB` is

.. math::

   P_{\mathrm{reac}}^{\mathrm{Bias}}
     = a\,\left(1 - \frac{E_d - E_v}{E_t + E_r}\right)
       \exp\!\left[\lambda\left(\frac{E_v}{E_d} - 1\right)\right],
       \qquad E_t + E_r + E_v > E_d,

where :math:`E_t` is the relative translational energy of the
pair, and :math:`E_v`, :math:`E_r` are the vibrational and
rotational energies of the dissociating molecule, taken directly
from the rovibronic :math:`(n, v, J)` energy database so that the
favoring is fully state-resolved. :math:`E_d` is the dissociation
energy (well depth :math:`D_0`) of the molecule's current
electronic state. The two parameters are the calibration constant
:math:`a` (of order unity, ``aCoeff``) and the vibrational-favoring
exponent :math:`\lambda` (``lambda``). At the threshold
:math:`E_t + E_r + E_v = E_d` the prefactor vanishes; the
exponential biases the reaction toward molecules in high
vibrational states, and :math:`\lambda = 0` recovers an
unfavored model.

Unlike TCE, the Bias model has **no closed-form equilibrium rate**:
the discrete internal modes preclude analytic integration, so the
resulting :math:`k(T)` is obtained *a posteriori* by fitting, and
:math:`(a, \lambda)` are calibration parameters rather than derived
constants. Recommended starting values for oxygen are
:math:`a \approx 4,\ \lambda = 2` for :math:`\mathrm{O_2 + O}` and
:math:`a \approx 2,\ \lambda = 4` for :math:`\mathrm{O_2 + O_2}`
[GimelsheinBias2017]_. The Bias model is intended for the
dissociation (and, by detailed balance, recombination) channels
only; exchange reactions should use the TCE or QK probability.

Post-reaction redistribution of internal energy
-----------------------------------------------

Once a reaction has fired, the pooled energy available to the
products is

.. math::

   E_{\mathrm{avail}} = E_c + \Delta H
                        - E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(n_P, v_P, J_P)},

where :math:`E_c = E_{\mathrm{rel}} + E_{\mathrm{int}}(P) +
E_{\mathrm{int}}(Q)` is the collision energy pool,
:math:`\Delta H` is the heat of reaction (automatically read
from the database for dissociation, recombination, and exchange;
cf. :doc:`../theory/chemistry`). The redistribution proceeds in
the order below.

**Product-state enumeration.** For a dissociation
:math:`\mathrm{AB}(n, v, J) + \mathrm{M} \to \mathrm{A}(n_1) +
\mathrm{B}(n_2) + \mathrm{M}`, the product atoms' electronic
states :math:`(n_1, n_2)` are determined at initialisation by
the asymptote-matching algorithm below. For an exchange
reaction, two molecular / atomic products must be sampled.

**Larsen-Borgnakke sampling for the first product.** The first
product's state :math:`(n', v', J')_{\mathrm{AC}}` is sampled by
acceptance-rejection,

.. math::

   f_{1}(E_{\mathrm{AC}}')
     = g(E_{\mathrm{AC}}')\,
       \left(\frac{E_{\mathrm{avail}}
                    - E_{\mathrm{AC}}'}{E_{\mathrm{avail}}}\right)^{\alpha_{1}},

with exponent

.. math::

   \alpha_{1}
     = \frac{\xi_{\mathrm{rel}} + \xi_{\mathrm{AC}}^{\mathrm{int}}
             + \xi_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{int}}}{2} - 1,

where the exponent pools the translational DOF *and* the internal
DOF of every product whose state has not yet been sampled.

**Energy update.** The remaining pool is

.. math::

   E_{\mathrm{avail}}' = E_{\mathrm{avail}} - E_{\mathrm{AC}}'.

**Second-product state.** The second product's state is sampled
in the same way, with exponent

.. math::

   \alpha_{2}
     = \frac{\xi_{\mathrm{rel}} + \xi_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{int}}}{2} - 1,

and energy :math:`E_{\mathrm{B}}'` drawn against :math:`E_{\mathrm{avail}}'`.

**Post-reaction translational energy.** The remaining pool,

.. math::

   E_{\mathrm{rel}}'
     = E_{\mathrm{avail}}' - E_{\mathrm{B}}',

becomes the translational kinetic energy of the products'
relative motion. The post-reaction relative speed is
:math:`g' = \sqrt{2 E_{\mathrm{rel}}'/\mu'}`, with :math:`\mu'`
the reduced mass of the products, and the product velocities
follow from the conservation laws applied to the product pair.

**Detailed balance.** The sequential acceptance-rejection above
reproduces the thermodynamically-weighted product-state
distribution at equilibrium, and the forward and backward rates
of any channel are connected through the equilibrium constant.
This is the key property that allows the recombination rate to
be derived parameter-free from the dissociation rate (below).

Dissociation with potential-energy-curve asymptote matching
-----------------------------------------------------------

When the molecule AB dissociates from electronic state :math:`n`,
the electronic states of the product atoms are determined by
**matching the PEC asymptote**. Define the asymptote energy

.. math::

   \mathrm{asymptote}(n) = T_e^{(n)} + E_d^{(n)} - E^{(0)}_{000},

and the excess asymptote

.. math::

   \Delta E(n) = \mathrm{asymptote}(n) - \mathrm{asymptote}(0).

For each molecular electronic state, we search over all feasible
pairs :math:`(n_1, n_2)` of product-atom electronic states for
the combination that minimises

.. math::

   \mathrm{err}(n_1, n_2)
     = \bigl|\,E_{A}(n_1) + E_{B}(n_2) - \Delta E(n)\,\bigr|.

The mapping :math:`n \mapsto (n_1^{\star}, n_2^{\star})` is built
at initialisation, stored in a look-up table, and printed to the
terminal together with its residual errors (typically smaller
than :math:`1~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}`). The match guarantees that the
Wigner-Witmer correlation rules are obeyed at the solver level,
without any user input.

Recombination from detailed balance
-----------------------------------

The three-body recombination rate
:math:`\mathrm{A} + \mathrm{B} + \mathrm{M} \to \mathrm{AB} +
\mathrm{M}` is not a free parameter. It is derived from the
dissociation rate through the equilibrium constant,

.. math::

   k^{\mathrm{rec}}(T) = \frac{k^{D}(T)}{K_{\mathrm{eq}}(T)},

with

.. math::

   K_{\mathrm{eq}}(T)
     = \frac{(2\pi m_A k_B T)^{3/2}\,(2\pi m_B k_B T)^{3/2}\,h^{3}}
            {(2\pi m_{\mathrm{AB}} k_B T)^{3/2}}\,
       \frac{Q_A(T)\,Q_B(T)}{Q_{\mathrm{AB}}(T)}\,
       \exp\!\left(-\frac{D_0}{k_B T}\right).

The partition functions :math:`Q_i(T)` are built at
initialisation from the rovibronic database, and
:math:`D_0` is read from the molecule's database entry. The
user supplies neither the recombination rate nor the heat of
reaction; both are computed internally. Run-time, a collision
between :math:`\mathrm{A}` and :math:`\mathrm{B}` in the
presence of a third body :math:`\mathrm{M}` is tested with
probability :math:`P_{\mathrm{rec}} = n_M k^{\mathrm{rec}}(T) /
\nu_{\mathrm{coll}}(\mathrm{A},\mathrm{B})`.

Atom-exchange (Zeldovich-type) reactions
----------------------------------------

For :math:`\mathrm{AB} + \mathrm{C} \to \mathrm{AC} + \mathrm{B}`,
the heat of reaction is computed automatically from the database
as

.. math::

   \Delta H_{\mathrm{exch}}
     = D_0(\mathrm{AC}) - D_0(\mathrm{AB}).

The QK reaction probability,

.. math::

   P_{\mathrm{exch}}^{\mathrm{QK}}(E_c; n, v, J)
     = \frac{\bigl[1 - E_{\mathrm{act}}^{(n,v,J)}/E_c\bigr]^{3/2 - \omega}}
            {\displaystyle\sum_{n',v',J'}^{\mathrm{BC}}
              g(n', v', J')\,
              \bigl[1 - E(n', v', J')/E_c\bigr]^{3/2 - \omega}},

normalises the state-resolved threshold by the Boltzmann-averaged
denominator over the product-molecule level space, and fires
through the two-stage LB sampling described above. Start-up
diagnostics print the computed heat against literature values:
:math:`\mathrm{N}_2 + \mathrm{O} \to \mathrm{NO} + \mathrm{N}`
returns :math:`\Delta H \approx -37\,732~\mathrm{K}`, within 1%
of the tabulated value.

Polyatomic chemistry
--------------------

The TCE and QK machinery above is extended to **polyatomic
reactants and products** —  the CO\ :sub:`2`, CH\ :sub:`4` and HCN
chemistries of Mars and Titan entry —  through a dedicated family
of reaction classes whose internal-energy accounting sums over
*all* oscillators of the molecule rather than a single vibrational
mode. The polyatomic collision energy is

.. math::

   E_c = E_{\mathrm{rel}}
       + \sum_{\text{reactants}}
         \Bigl[E_{\mathrm{elec}} + E_{\mathrm{rot}}
               + \textstyle\sum_m E_{\mathrm{vib},m}\Bigr],

with the multi-mode vibrational sum taken over the direct-product
ladder of :doc:`rovibronic_levels`. The classes follow the
polyatomic-reaction model of Nizenkov, Pfeiffer and co-workers
[Nizenkov2017_ch]_.

* ``polyDissociationTCE`` —  TCE dissociation of a polyatomic,
  :math:`\mathrm{AB}_2 + \mathrm{M} \to \mathrm{AB} + \mathrm{B} +
  \mathrm{M}`. The forward probability is the polyatomic TCE form,
  calibrated to reproduce the Arrhenius rate
  :math:`k(T) = A_f T^{b_f}\exp(-T_a/T)`; it is algebraically
  identical to the verified diatomic TCE in the single-oscillator
  limit. Two thresholds must both be met —  the reactant must carry
  more than the activation energy, and the product channel must be
  energetically open.

* ``polyDissociationQK`` —  parameter-free QK dissociation: the
  bond breaks when :math:`E_{\mathrm{rel}} + E_{\mathrm{int}}` of
  the polyatomic exceeds the bond energy :math:`D_0`, with
  :math:`E_{\mathrm{int}}` the full electronic + rotational +
  all-oscillator internal energy. Only :math:`D_0` is required.

* ``polyExchangeTCE`` —  TCE atom exchange
  :math:`\mathrm{A} + \mathrm{BC} \leftrightarrow \mathrm{AB} +
  \mathrm{C}` in which the reactant and/or the product is
  polyatomic (e.g. :math:`\mathrm{CO}_2 + \mathrm{O}
  \leftrightarrow \mathrm{CO} + \mathrm{O}_2`). A ``rateMethod``
  key selects the ``arrhenius`` (forward) probability or the
  ``detailedBalance`` (reverse) prefactor.

* ``polyRecombinationTCE`` / ``polyRecombinationQK`` —  the
  detailed-balance partners of the two dissociation classes, with
  the backward rate obtained from the equilibrium constant (below).

Backward rates from the equilibrium constant
````````````````````````````````````````````

Every reverse (recombination / reverse-exchange) channel obtains
its rate from the forward rate through the equilibrium constant,
:math:`k_b(T) = k_f(T)/K_{\mathrm{eq}}(T)`, recomputed once per
time-step from the local translational temperature. The
concentration equilibrium constant is built from the species
partition functions,

.. math::

   K_{\mathrm{eq}}(T)
     = \frac{q_{\mathrm{AB}}\,q_{\mathrm{B}}}{q_{\mathrm{AB}_2}}\,
       \exp\!\left(\frac{\Delta E_{\mathrm{form}}}{k_B T}\right),
   \qquad
   q_i = q_i^{\mathrm{tr}}\,q_i^{\mathrm{rot}}\,
         q_i^{\mathrm{vib}}\,q_i^{\mathrm{elec}},

with the rotational sum taken over the stored ladder, the
vibrational factor a product over the molecule's oscillators, and
the electronic sum over the term energies. A subtle but important
point of the implementation is that the barrierless backward
prefactor must be built as
:math:`\beta_b(T) = \beta_{\mathrm{pre}}\,e^{-T_a/T}/K_{\mathrm{eq}}(T)`
rather than by dividing the forward prefactor by
:math:`K_{\mathrm{eq}}`: the collision-energy average of the
forward shape factor is :math:`A_f T^{b_f}` (no exponential), so
reusing :math:`\beta_f/K_{\mathrm{eq}}` would *not* satisfy
detailed balance. This exact-:math:`\beta_b` construction is
verified by the ``CO2_exchange_detbal`` and ``CH4_CH3_detbal``
cases.

The methane dissociation chain
``````````````````````````````

A representative application is the **methane pyrolysis cascade**
of Titan entry,
:math:`\mathrm{CH}_4 \to \mathrm{CH}_3 \to \mathrm{CH}_2 \to
\mathrm{CH} \to \mathrm{C}`, in which each step strips one hydrogen
through a ``polyDissociationTCE`` reaction (the final
:math:`\mathrm{CH} \to \mathrm{C} + \mathrm{H}` step uses the
diatomic ``dissociationTCE``). The chain is configured in
``run/0D/CH4_dissoc_chain``; its bond energies are taken from the
Active Thermochemical Tables, and the forward steps use a
collision-theory bond-energy rate
:math:`k(T) = A\,T^{1/2}\exp(-D_0/k_B T)` rather than the measured
low-pressure-limit fall-off rate, whose steep negative exponent
would otherwise stall the cascade above
:math:`\sim 8000~\mathrm{K}`. The measured Baulch rate
[Baulch2005]_ is verified separately in
``run/0D/CH4_to_CH3_rate``.

Joint post-reaction redistribution
``````````````````````````````````

When a polyatomic reaction fires, the product molecule's rotation
*and every oscillator* are resampled jointly from the
Bergemann-Boyd discrete Larsen-Borgnakke kernel
[BergemannBoyd1994]_, with the relaxation collision numbers forced
to unity so that the nascent product is fully equilibrated. The
residual energy becomes the fragments' relative translational
energy, and total momentum is conserved.

Internal-DOF accounting in the TCE prefactor (``xiCMode``)
``````````````````````````````````````````````````````````

The TCE reaction probability depends on the average internal
degrees of freedom :math:`\xi_{\mathrm{int}}` of the colliding pair
through the exponent and the :math:`\Gamma`-function prefactor. For
polyatomic dissociation and exchange, the ``xiCMode`` key selects
how :math:`\xi_{\mathrm{int}}` is evaluated:

* ``cellThermal`` (default) —  the cell-local, temperature-
  consistent caloric effective DOF. This is the convention under
  which the TCE prefactor is calibrated, and it reproduces the
  analytic Arrhenius rate; it is the correct choice for
  quantitative rates.
* ``perCollision`` —  the instantaneous per-collision DOF from the
  occupied :math:`(n, v, J)` state (the literal choice of the
  underlying paper), useful for cross-comparison but mismatched
  with the prefactor calibration.
* ``shape`` —  an opt-in distribution-shape vibrational DOF, the
  density-of-states log-slope at the activation energy, intended
  for strong vibrational non-equilibrium
  (:math:`T_{\mathrm{vib}} \ll T_{\mathrm{trans}}`).

Chemistry models at a glance
----------------------------

The solver provides four families of gas-phase chemistry models,
selectable per reaction (or, for FHO, as a binary-collision model):

* **TCE** —  Bird's total-collision-energy model, Arrhenius-
  calibrated, available for dissociation, exchange, ionisation and
  recombination, with polyatomic variants above.
* **QK** —  Bird's parameter-free Quantum-Kinetic model, requiring
  only the bond / ionisation energy, with polyatomic variants.
* **Bias** —  the Gimelshein-Wysong vibrationally-favored
  dissociation model documented above.
* **FHO** —  the Forced Harmonic Oscillator vibration-translation
  model of Adamovich, documented as a collision model in
  :doc:`relaxation`; it predicts state-to-state vibrational
  transition probabilities, including multi-quantum jumps, rather
  than a relaxation rate.
