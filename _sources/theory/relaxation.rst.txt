Internal-energy relaxation
==========================

We now document the relaxation of the rotational, vibrational,
and electronic internal modes of the molecular species toward
equilibrium with the translational mode. The present section
derives the Landau-Teller rate equation from first principles,
describes the Millikan-White and Parker empirical correlations
from which the relaxation collision numbers are read, introduces
the Nizenkov correction that guarantees rate-matching in a
multi-mode cascade, and documents the parallel-marginal cascade
by which the solver turns the analytic rate equations into an
event-by-event sampler on the discrete rovibronic state space.

Landau-Teller rate equation
---------------------------

Under the assumption of an exponential decay of correlations, the
mean energy :math:`\langle E_k\rangle` of internal mode :math:`k`
approaches its equilibrium value :math:`\langle E_k\rangle(T_T)`
at the translational temperature :math:`T_T` according to

.. math::

   \frac{\mathrm{d}\,\langle E_k\rangle}{\mathrm{d}t}
     = \frac{\langle E_k\rangle(T_T) - \langle E_k\rangle(t)}
            {\tau_k},

where :math:`\tau_k` is the relaxation time of mode :math:`k`.
The Landau-Teller form

.. math::

   \tau_k = Z_k^{\mathrm{cont}}\,\tau_c,

with :math:`\tau_c = 1/\nu_{\mathrm{coll}}` the mean collision
time, relates the continuum-level relaxation time to a
**collision number** :math:`Z_k^{\mathrm{cont}}`, i.e. the mean
number of collisions required to equilibrate the mode. Under
isothermal heat-bath conditions the equation admits the explicit
solution

.. math::

   \langle E_k\rangle(t)
     = \langle E_k\rangle(T_T)
       - \bigl[\langle E_k\rangle(T_T) - \langle E_k\rangle(0)\bigr]\,
         \exp\!\left(-\frac{t}{\tau_k}\right),

which is the reference curve against which the solver is
validated (:doc:`../examples/relaxation_landau_teller`).

Multi-mode decomposition
------------------------

When several internal modes relax on well-separated timescales
:math:`\tau_R \ll \tau_{RV} \ll \tau_{RVE}`, the total internal
energy approaches equilibrium as a sum of exponentials:

.. math::

   \langle E_{\mathrm{int}}\rangle(t)
     = \langle E_{\mathrm{int}}\rangle^{\star}
     - \Delta E_R\, e^{-t/\tau_R}
     - \Delta E_V\, e^{-t/\tau_{RV}}
     - \Delta E_E\, e^{-t/\tau_{RVE}},

where :math:`\Delta E_k` is the initial energy deficit of mode
:math:`k`. In normalised form,

.. math::

   \frac{\langle E_{\mathrm{int}}\rangle^{\star}
         - \langle E_{\mathrm{int}}\rangle(t)}
        {\langle E_{\mathrm{int}}\rangle^{\star}
         - \langle E_{\mathrm{int}}\rangle(0)}
     = A_R\, e^{-t/\tau_R}
     + A_V\, e^{-t/\tau_{RV}}
     + A_E\, e^{-t/\tau_{RVE}},

with :math:`A_k = \Delta E_k / \Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}` and
:math:`A_R + A_V + A_E = 1`.

Millikan-White and Parker correlations
--------------------------------------

The rotational collision number :math:`Z_r(T)` is fitted, in the
manner of Parker [Parker1959rel]_, to measured rotational-
translational thermalisation data:

.. math::

   Z_r(T)
     = \frac{Z_r^{\infty}}{1 + (\pi^{3/2}/2)\,\sqrt{\theta_r^{\star}/T}
                           + (\pi + \pi^{2}/4)\,(\theta_r^{\star}/T)},

with :math:`\theta_r^{\star}` a reference rotational temperature
and :math:`Z_r^{\infty}` an asymptotic value that is typically
fitted to 5–10 for air diatomics. In the DSMC implementation the
low-temperature asymptote is tabulated directly as
``rotationalRelaxationCollisionNumber`` in the
``<Model>Coeffs`` sub-dictionary.

The vibrational relaxation time follows the Millikan-White
correlation [MillikanWhite1963rel]_:

.. math::

   p\,\tau_v^{\mathrm{MW}}(T)
     = \exp\!\left[A\,\bigl(T^{-1/3} - B\bigr) - 18.42\right],
   \qquad \text{[atm}\cdot\text{s]},

with :math:`A = 1.16 \times 10^{-3}\,\mu^{1/2}\,\theta_v^{4/3}`
and :math:`B = 0.015\,\mu^{1/4}`, the reduced mass :math:`\mu` in
atomic units, and the characteristic vibrational temperature
:math:`\theta_v` in kelvin. Park's high-temperature correction,
:math:`\tau_v^{\mathrm{Park}} = \tau_v^{\mathrm{MW}} +
1/(\sigma_v\,c_s\,n_s)`, brings the relaxation rate under control
above :math:`10^{4}~\mathrm{K}`, where the Millikan-White
exponential extrapolation would otherwise produce unphysically
fast relaxation. In the DSMC implementation, the effective
vibrational collision number
``rovibrationalRelaxationCollisionNumber`` absorbs the combined
Millikan-White/Park relation, and the database-derived
:math:`\tau_v(T)` is checked against the input at solver
initialisation.

Electronic relaxation times are less well characterised
experimentally than rotational or vibrational ones, and the
effective ``rovibronicRelaxationCollisionNumber`` in
``<Model>Coeffs`` is conventionally set to a large value
(:math:`10^{3}\text{–}10^{4}`) that reflects the slow equilibration
typical of atomic emission observations. Setting it to
:math:`\infty` amounts to freezing the electronic populations —
useful diagnostically, but physically justified only in short
transient cases.

Temperature-dependent, partner-dependent vibrational :math:`Z_v(T)`
-------------------------------------------------------------------

The constant relaxation collision numbers above can be replaced by
a **temperature-dependent vibrational collision number**
:math:`Z_v(T)`, evaluated per collision in a generalised
Millikan-White form with a Park high-temperature limiter. The
effective temperature entering the correlation is reconstructed,
per collision, from the collision invariants (the detailed-balance
choice of Eckert and Gallis [EckertGallis2022]_),

.. math::

   T = \frac{E_{\mathrm{trans}} + E_{\mathrm{mode}}}
            {k_B\,\xi}, \qquad
   \xi = \max\!\left(\tfrac{5}{2} - \omega + \zeta_{\mathrm{mode}},\;
                     \epsilon\right),

and the collision number is

.. math::

   Z_v(T) = \frac{B_1}{T^{\omega}}\,
            \exp\!\left(\frac{B_2}{T^{1/3}}\right)
          + \frac{B_1}{T_{\mathrm{HT}}^{\omega}}\,
            \exp\!\left(\frac{B_2}{T_{\mathrm{HT}}^{1/3}}\right).

The first term is the Millikan-White correlation
[MillikanWhite1963rel]_ written directly as a collision number with
calibration constants :math:`(B_1, B_2)`; the second term is the
Park high-temperature floor [Park1989rel]_ —  the same expression
frozen at the limiting temperature :math:`T_{\mathrm{HT}}`, which
prevents the Millikan-White exponential from collapsing
:math:`Z_v` to unphysically small values above :math:`10^{4}~`K.
The global defaults :math:`(B_1, B_2, T_{\mathrm{HT}})` are read
from the keys ``vibB1``, ``vibB2`` and ``vibTHT`` in
``collisionProperties``; if ``vibB1`` is left at zero the channel
returns an effectively infinite :math:`Z_v` and remains inert.

**Partner dependence.** Because the Millikan-White constants depend
on the reduced mass and the interaction of the colliding pair, the
same vibrator relaxes at very different rates against different
partners (for instance N\ :sub:`2` relaxes several times faster
against atomic O than against Ar). Following Valentini
[Valentini2026]_, the solver therefore allows a per-pair table of
:math:`(B_1, B_2, T_{\mathrm{HT}})`, supplied as the nested
sub-dictionary ``vibrationalRelaxationData`` keyed by the vibrating
species :math:`P` and its collision partner :math:`Q`. Each pair is
first initialised to the global defaults; a per-relaxer ``default``
block then overrides all partners of a given vibrator, and explicit
partner blocks override individual pairs. A single-pair calibration
with no overrides reproduces the global behaviour exactly.

**Model and channel selectors.** Two keys in
``collisionProperties`` control the model:

* ``vibrationalRelaxationModel`` —  ``constant`` keeps the legacy
  constant collision number; ``millikanWhite`` and ``valentini``
  activate the temperature-dependent :math:`Z_v(T)` above (the
  ``valentini`` value additionally consults the per-pair table). If
  the key is absent, :math:`Z_v(T)` is auto-enabled whenever
  ``vibB1`` is positive.
* ``vibrationalRelaxationChannel`` —  ``rovib`` (default) routes
  :math:`Z_v(T)` to the rovibrational :math:`(v, J)` channel and
  reproduces the prior behaviour byte for byte; ``pureVib`` routes
  it to the pure-vibrational :math:`v`-only (V-T) channel, leaving
  the rovibrational channel on its constant collision number.
  Routing :math:`Z_v(T)` to exactly one of the two mutually
  exclusive channels avoids double-counting the V-T rate.

The pure-vibrational channel and the partner-dependent
:math:`Z_v(T)` are exercised by the verification cases under
``run/0D/`` —  ``N2_pureVT_relax`` (rotation frozen, pure V-T
relaxation), ``N2_pureVT_detbal`` (V-T detailed balance), and the
``N2O_pureVT_valentini`` / ``N2Ar_pureVT_valentini`` pair, which
confirm the partner-dependent rate ratio.

Composite modes in the parallel-marginal cascade
-------------------------------------------------

Rather than redistributing energy independently between each
internal mode and the translational pool, the solver groups the
relaxation events into four composite modes organised by the
sub-space of the rovibronic state that they sample:

* mode :math:`R` (rotational-only) samples :math:`J` at fixed
  :math:`(n, v)`; the mode energy is measured relative to the
  floor :math:`E_R^{\mathrm{floor}} = E(n, v, 0)`;
* mode :math:`V` (pure-vibrational, V-T) samples :math:`v` at
  fixed :math:`(n, J)`; this is the genuine vibration-translation
  channel, isolated from rotation, and is the natural target of
  the Millikan-White vibrational relaxation time;
* mode :math:`RV` (rovibrational) samples :math:`(v, J)` at fixed
  :math:`n`; floor :math:`E_{RV}^{\mathrm{floor}} = E(n, 0, 0)`;
* mode :math:`RVE` (rovibronic) samples :math:`(n, v, J)` on the
  full table; floor :math:`E_{RVE}^{\mathrm{floor}} = 0`.

The pure-vibrational channel is switched off by default (its
collision number is initialised to a very large value) and becomes
active only when a ``vibrationalRelaxationCollisionNumber`` is
supplied, or when a temperature-dependent :math:`Z_v(T)` is routed
to it (see below). The two routes —  the pure-vibrational
:math:`v`-only channel and the rovibrational :math:`(v, J)` channel
—  are mutually exclusive window events, so vibrational energy is
never double-counted.

Each composite mode carries its own effective degrees of
freedom, computed from the sub-space partition function,

.. math::

   \xi_k(T)
     = \frac{2\,\langle E_k\rangle(T)}{k_B\,T},

and these :math:`\xi_k(T)` feed the Larsen-Borgnakke exponent
(:doc:`larsen_borgnakke`).

Event selection
---------------

On each accepted collision the solver draws a single uniform
:math:`R \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)` and selects one of five
**mutually exclusive** events from the cumulative marginals:

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   P_1 &= \frac{1}{Z_r\,C_r}, \\
   P_v &= \frac{1}{Z_v\,C_v}, \\
   P_2 &= \frac{1}{Z_{rv}\,C_{rv}}, \\
   P_3 &= \frac{1}{Z_{rve}\,C_{rve}}, \\
   P_0 &= 1 - P_1 - P_v - P_2 - P_3,
   \end{aligned}

where event :math:`E_1` triggers rotational-only sampling,
:math:`E_v` pure-vibrational (V-T) sampling, :math:`E_2`
rovibrational sampling, :math:`E_3` rovibronic sampling, and
:math:`E_0` leaves the internal state unchanged (elastic-like
event). The window is evaluated as
:math:`E_1 : R < P_1`,
:math:`E_v : P_1 \le R < P_1 + P_v`, and so on. When
:math:`P_1 + P_v + P_2 + P_3 > 1` the four probabilities are
uniformly rescaled to saturate, and a warning is emitted in the
solver log: the effective collision numbers seen by the simulation
then differ from the input values.

Nizenkov correction
-------------------

The collision numbers :math:`Z_k` are defined at the continuum
level, while the cascade acts at the event level. The two are
related by a temperature-dependent correction factor derived by
Nizenkov [Pfeiffer2016rel]_:

.. math::

   C_k
     = \frac{\xi_T\,(T_T - T'_k)}
            {\xi_k(T_T)\,T_T - \xi_k(T_k)\,T_k},

where :math:`\xi_T` is the translational DOF entering the
exchange, and :math:`T'_k` is the pool temperature that
satisfies local energy conservation in the cascade event. Without
the :math:`C_k` factors, the macroscopic relaxation observed by
the solver drifts systematically from the analytical
Landau-Teller solution; with them in place, the ratio
:math:`Z_{k,\mathrm{fit}}/Z_k` recovers the input to within a few
percent on the validation cases. A derivation of
:math:`C_k` is given in ``docs/derivations/LT_Correction_MultiMode.pdf``.

Each channel carries its **own** correction, built per cell from
that channel's conditional manifold: :math:`C_r` from the
:math:`J`-ladder at fixed :math:`(n, v)`, :math:`C_v` from the
:math:`v`-ladder at fixed :math:`(n, J)` (the states the
pure-vibrational event actually samples), :math:`C_{rv}` from the
:math:`(v, J)` manifold at fixed :math:`n`, and :math:`C_{rve}`
from the full spin-allowed :math:`(n, v, J)` manifold. The
channel temperature :math:`T_k` is inverted from the measured
per-cell energy of that same conditional manifold. (An earlier
implementation borrowed the rovibrational :math:`C_{rv}` for the
pure-vibrational channel; this mis-scaled the constant-:math:`Z_v`
V-T rate — for the N2 pure-V-T verification case by
:math:`C_{rv}/C_v \approx 0.72` — and has been fixed.)

Phase-by-phase validation protocol
----------------------------------

The cascade is validated phase by phase by sending the collision
numbers of all but one mode to a very large value,
:math:`Z_j \to 10^{9}` for :math:`j \ne k`, and comparing the
observed relaxation curve against the analytical single-mode LT
solution.

* **Phase 1** —  rotational only: freeze
  :math:`Z_{rv}, Z_{rve}`. Initial state :math:`T_T =
  10^{4}~\mathrm{K}`, :math:`T_R(0) = 10^{3}~\mathrm{K}`. The
  fitted :math:`Z_r` should match the input within 5%. At the
  time of writing the ratio is :math:`Z_{\mathrm{fit}}/Z \approx
  1.07`, acceptable.
* **Phase 2** —  rovibrational: freeze :math:`Z_r, Z_{rve}`.
  Expected: rotational sub-ladder equilibrates near
  instantaneously (because rotational events are frozen, the
  cascade has only one active mode), followed by vibrational
  relaxation. Current ratio :math:`\approx 0.83`; the phase
  overcorrects and is under investigation.
* **Phase 3** —  all modes active. Expected triple-exponential
  decay; ratio pending diagnosis.

On the absence of the classic ZCorr
-----------------------------------

The continuous-variable implementations of Larsen-Borgnakke
require the so-called ZCorr correction to match the macroscopic
Landau-Teller rate (since, in the continuous formulation, the
effective DOF enter the exponent while the sampled population is
unphysically concentrated near the mean). In the rovibronic
extension the sampled population is the *actual* quantum
distribution, and a direct measurement
[Civrais2024Vib_rel]_ of :math:`Z_{\mathrm{fit}}/Z_{\mathrm{input}}`
returns :math:`\approx 0.99` without any correction factor,
confirming that **ZCorr is not required** on the rovibronic
Larsen-Borgnakke path. The relevant diagnostic history is
tracked in the in-repo bug-audit report.

Forced Harmonic Oscillator (FHO-FR) model
-----------------------------------------

As an alternative to the Larsen-Borgnakke vibrational channel,
the solver provides the **Forced Harmonic Oscillator** model of
Adamovich et al. [Adamovich1998rel]_, in its free-rotation
(FHO-FR) form, selectable through
``BinaryCollisionModel  FHOVariableHardSphere;`` in
``constant/dsmcProperties``. It is a physically based,
non-perturbative model that — unlike the empirical Landau-Teller
collision number — predicts the full set of state-to-state
vibrational transition probabilities, including multi-quantum
jumps, from the intermolecular potential.

When this model is selected, rotation and electronic modes still
relax by the Larsen-Borgnakke cascade above (their vibrational,
rovibrational and rovibronic channels are frozen), but the
vibrational transition :math:`v \to w` of each molecular partner
is drawn from the analytic FHO probability

.. math::

   P_{\mathrm{VT}}(v \to w)
     = \frac{n_s^{\,s}}{(s!)^2}\, Q^{\,s}\,
       \exp\!\left[ -\frac{2 n_s}{s+1} Q
                    - \frac{n_s^2}{(s+1)^2 (s+2)} Q^2 \right],
   \qquad s = |v - w|,

with :math:`n_s = (\max(v,w)!/\min(v,w)!)^{1/s}` and the
trajectory integral

.. math::

   Q = \frac{\theta'}{4\,\theta}\,
       \frac{1}{\sinh^2\!\left( \pi\omega/(\alpha\,u\,\gamma) \right)},
   \quad
   \theta' = \frac{4\pi^2 \omega^2 \mu}{\alpha^2 k_B},
   \quad
   \theta = \frac{\hbar\omega}{k_B},
   \quad
   u = \sqrt{2 E_t/\mu},

where :math:`\omega = |E_w - E_v|/(s\hbar)` is the average
vibrational quantum of the transition, :math:`\alpha` the
repulsive-potential range parameter (``alpha``, default
:math:`4.0\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}`), :math:`\mu` the
reduced mass, and :math:`E_t` the relative translational energy.
The orientation factor

.. math::

   \gamma = \max\!\left[ 0,\;
     \sqrt{(1 - E_r/E_t)(1 - (b/R_0)^2)}
     - \tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2\vartheta)\cos(\varphi)\sqrt{E_r/E_t}
     \right]

embodies the FHO-FR free-rotation averaging: the impact parameter
:math:`b \in [0, R_0]` and rotation phase angles
:math:`\vartheta \in [0,\pi]`, :math:`\varphi \in [0,\pi/2]` are
sampled per collision, :math:`E_r` is the molecule's rotational
energy, and the closest-approach distance is
:math:`R_0(E_t) = 2.5 - (1/2\alpha)\ln(E_t[\mathrm{cm^{-1}}]/10^4)`
Å. The candidate final levels :math:`w` are enumerated subject to
energy conservation, their probabilities accumulated, and the new
level sampled (with the complementary probability assigned to "no
transition"). In the collinear, non-rotating limit
(:math:`\gamma = 1`) the single-quantum probability reduces to the
classical SSH result, recovering Landau-Teller behaviour at low
collision energy.
