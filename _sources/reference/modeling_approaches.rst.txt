Data provenance and modelling approaches
========================================

Two sets of reference documents in the source tree underpin the
trustworthiness of the solver: the provenance records of every
numerical constant, and a cross-code comparison of the modelling
choices against two established DSMC codes. This page summarises
both.

Data provenance
---------------

**Species data.** Masses, collision parameters and spectroscopic
constants form the frozen species database. They are generated
from source files stored in
``src/lagrangian/DSMC/speciesDatabase/sources/``, and each value is
written together with its source into
``species_properties_frozen.json`` and
``species_spectroscopy_frozen.json``. The rules, the NON-NIST flags
and the dropped states are recorded in ``sources/README.md`` and
``sources/nist/BUILD_REPORT.md``; the page
:doc:`../classes/species_database_sources` summarises them.

**Other constants.** ``docs/Development/DATA_PROVENANCE.md`` maps
the remaining numerical constants to their sources —  author,
journal or database, table or CAS number, DOI —  and to their
location in the code. It is descriptive only: where a code value
disagrees with its nominal source, the discrepancy is *recorded*,
not silently edited. Its species sections (spectroscopic constants,
VHS/VSS parameters, dissociation energies) predate the frozen
database and are superseded by the generated records above; the
sections that remain current cover:

* chemistry reaction rates (Arrhenius :math:`A`, :math:`\eta`,
  :math:`T_a`) with the per-mole ↔ per-particle unit conversion
  rule;
* relaxation and collision-model constants (Millikan-White, Park
  limiter, Eckert-Gallis, the Z-correction, the per-oscillator
  effective DOF, Bergemann-Boyd);
* GSI surface-reaction rates;
* flagged discrepancies and uncertain values (e.g. the
  order-of-magnitude scatter in measured GSI rates).

Cross-code modelling comparison
-------------------------------

``docs/Development/MODELING_APPROACHES.md`` compares the modelling choices of
the present solver against **SPARTA** and **PICLas**, read from
each code's own source. The comparison is intended both as a guard
against subtle modelling drift and as a guide to which code is the
appropriate reference for a given physics question. The principal
differences are summarised below.

Chemistry
`````````

* **Collision energy content.** SPARTA pools translation and
  rotation (vibration only in its *full* mode) and carries no
  electronic energy; PICLas pools the per-collision internal
  energy; the present solver always pools translation, rotation,
  *all* vibrational modes and electronic energy from a discrete
  level table.
* **TCE internal DOF.** SPARTA and PICLas use the instantaneous
  per-collision DOF; the present solver defaults to the cell-
  thermal effective DOF (rate-calibrated to better than 0.1 %),
  with the per-collision and distribution-shape options available
  opt-in (``xiCMode``).
* **Backward reactions.** SPARTA has no equilibrium constant
  (reverse reactions are independent user Arrhenius rates); PICLas
  uses :math:`k_b = k_f/K_{\mathrm{eq}}` with a closed-form
  harmonic-oscillator :math:`K_{\mathrm{eq}}`; the present solver
  builds :math:`K_{\mathrm{eq}}` from the *actual* anharmonic
  ladders with the exact barrierless backward prefactor.
* **Polyatomic chemistry.** The present solver provides dedicated
  ``poly{Diss,Exch,Recomb}{TCE,QK}`` classes and the methane
  chain; the Gimelshein-Wysong vibrationally-favored Bias model is
  unique to this solver.

Internal energy and relaxation
``````````````````````````````

* **Vibration.** SPARTA uses a harmonic discrete ladder; PICLas
  uses harmonic by default (Morse for diatomics only); the present
  solver is always anharmonic, with opt-in cross-mode coupling and
  per-mode degeneracy.
* **Rotation.** SPARTA is continuous, PICLas quantised; the present
  solver uses discrete levels with nuclear-spin parity and the
  :math:`(J, K)` ladder for tops.
* **Vibrational** :math:`Z_v`. SPARTA offers constant or
  Millikan-White (no high-T floor), PICLas Millikan-White with a
  Park limiter; the present solver uses the generalised
  Millikan-White with the Park limiter, the Eckert-Gallis
  collision-invariant temperature, and a partner-dependent table.
* **Electronic.** SPARTA has none; PICLas is quantised; the present
  solver carries quantised electronic states with a spin-
  conservation gate.

Other subsystems
````````````````

* **Ionisation.** PICLas leads on multiple ionisation, photo-
  ionisation, cross-section data and the full PIC field solve; the
  present solver carries a rich single-ionisation QK suite with
  free-electron parcels (multiple ionisation is a roadmap item).
* **Surface chemistry.** SPARTA is richer on coverage / site-state
  kinetics; the present solver is richer on finite-rate carbon
  ablation (explicit solid-carbon consumption, recession mass
  balance, recombination-energy accommodation), and additionally
  provides Kisliuk sticking and :math:`\gamma(T)` catalysis.
* **FHO.** The Adamovich Forced Harmonic Oscillator V-T model is
  unique to the present solver.

In short, the present solver is the most general of the three in
full state-resolved rovibronic chemistry and relaxation,
partition-function detailed-balance backward reactions, anharmonic
and coupled polyatomic vibration with electronic excitation, the
methane chain, and the FHO and Bias models; PICLas leads on
plasma / ionisation physics, and SPARTA's QCT variant leads on
ab-initio state-to-state rates and surface site-state kinetics.
