Conjugate solid: conduction, melting, film and droplets
=======================================================

**Verified:** the conducting solid of ``dsmcFoamAblation`` coupled to
the DSMC wall (transient conduction on a moving mesh, re-radiation,
Stefan melting with the melt removed as it forms), and the melt
film and droplets (lubrication film driven by the gas shear and
pressure, capillarity and a body force; refreezing; shedding;
free-molecular droplets). **Against:** exact solutions of 1-D
melting ablation, the shear-driven film, capillary levelling and
Neumann freezing, and the free-molecular ODE of a droplet. The
numbers below are those of the original single-region runs; the
multi-region layout reproduces them to round-off
(:doc:`b8_conjugate_multiregion`).

1-D melting ablation
--------------------

Aluminium slab (:math:`\rho` 2700, :math:`c_p` 900, :math:`k` 237,
:math:`T_m` 933.47 K, :math:`L` = 3.97e5 J/kg, :math:`T_0` 300 K)
under :math:`q` = 10\ :sup:`7` W/m² (``prescribedHeatFlux``,
:math:`\varepsilon = 0`), the coupled path running as in a real case.
Exact references for a semi-infinite slab: melt onset
:math:`t_m = \pi k\rho c (T_m - T_0)^2/(4q^2)` = 1.815 s, steady speed
:math:`v = q/(\rho(L + c(T_m - T_0)))` = 3.8296 mm/s.

   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 50 50

   * - Check
     - Result
   * - Surface temperature before melting against
       :math:`2q\sqrt{t/(\pi k\rho c)}`
     - within 1.6 K (of a 633 K rise)
   * - Melt onset
     - in the window (1.8, 1.9] s, exact 1.815 s
   * - Melting speed / exact steady speed
     - 0.9966 at 40 s, 0.99996 at 120 s

     - within 0.03 K over 120 mm

Aluminium cylinder melting in a rarefied O stream
-------------------------------------------------

The body-fitted cylinder (R = 0.5 mm, atomic O at 4 km/s, 1000 K,
2e22 m\ :sup:`-3`) coupled to a matching aluminium disk,
:math:`\varepsilon` = 0.1. The cylinder heats as a lumped body: its
mean temperature rises at 6431 K/s against 6434 K/s predicted by the
window-averaged DSMC heat flux (ratio 0.9996). Melting starts at the
stagnation point at 96 ms; on the front faces
:math:`q` = 11.05 MW/m² gives a melting speed of 10.31 mm/s
:math:`= (q - \varepsilon\sigma T_m^4)/(\rho L)`. The energy ledger
of the solid closes to 4e-12.

Melt film and droplets
----------------------

   :header-rows: 1

   * - Test
     - Reference

   * - Shear-driven film on a melting strip (:math:`\tau` = 10\ :sup:`3`
       Pa), 4 s
     - :math:`h = (2\mu S x/\tau)^{1/2}`
     - :math:`h`/exact at the downstream face edges 0.99998-1.0001
       (50 faces), 0.9999-1.0004 (100)
   * - Capillary levelling of :math:`h_0 + a\cos kx`
     - :math:`a_0\exp(-\sigma h_0^3 k^4 t/3\mu)`
     - rate 0.993 (:math:`a_0/h_0` = 0.1) and 0.994 (0.001)
   * - 20 µm film freezing onto aluminium at 900 K
     - Neumann, :math:`s = 2\lambda(\alpha t)^{1/2}`
     - 0.995-0.9995; all frozen by 0.50 ms (exact 0.475 ms)
   * - 10 µm droplet released in the DSMC O stream
     - free-molecular sphere ODE
     - 0.998-1.004 at 100 µs; position within 0.5 %

     - right-face film 0.98-0.985 of both faces' melt

Coupled demonstration with a film (body held and in free flight,
stopped at 0.2 mm of front recession, per metre of cylinder at
116 ms): melted 536 and 616 mg/m; on the wall as liquid 42 % and
97 %; refrozen 4 % and 3 %; energy closure 2e-12 and 3e-12. The shed
fraction of the held body (57 %) is not a result: the rear
accumulation reaches film Reynolds numbers of 600-700, outside the
lubrication model.

Run it yourself
---------------

The 1-D melting slab, the shear-driven film and the droplet in the DSMC
stream are tutorials (:doc:`../../guide/verification_tutorials`):
``tutorials/verification/gsi/B8_melt1D`` (exact solution, a few
seconds), ``B8_meltFilmShear`` (exact film thickness, about 30 s) and
``B8_dropletStream`` (free-molecular ODE, statistical tolerance from
five seeds, about 2 min), all on 1 core. Run ``./Allrun`` in the
tutorial directory: the check prints PASS or FAIL per metric, ends with
``PASS: n/n metrics pass`` and draws the comparison in ``plots/``.

``analyse_b8b.py``, ``analyse_corner.py`` (``MODE=corner``),
